For anyone new to this

AI learns from examples.

Raw data alone doesn't tell an AI model what's correct. A photo, a sentence or a recording has no built-in meaning to a machine.

Annotation adds that meaning — marking what's in an image, what a sentence intends, or whether a model's answer is good or bad.

Quality checks make that meaning consistent, so the same object, intent or answer is labeled the same way every time.

The result is structured, training-ready data that helps an AI model learn faster and perform more reliably.

Data for AGI & Generative AI

Training data for the next generation of AI.

Modern AI data work isn't limited to drawing boxes around images. Language and generative models need people to judge, rank and correct their outputs — not just label pictures.

AI systems need more than large datasets. They need accurate, relevant and human-validated examples that reflect how people actually use AI.

Data for Automotive AI

Annotated visual and sensor data for advanced driver systems.

Automotive AI systems need to recognize roads, vehicles, pedestrians and lane markings before they can support driver assistance and safety features. That starts with carefully annotated 2D and 3D data.

We annotate camera, video and LiDAR data so automotive models can be trained and evaluated against real, labeled scenes.

Data for Robotics & Physical AI

Real-world data for AI systems that act in physical space.

Physical AI — robots, arms and autonomous systems — needs to understand the environment it operates in, not just images on a screen. That means combining visual, spatial and motion data.

We label RGB, depth, LiDAR and sensor data so robotics teams can train and evaluate how their systems perceive and interact with the world.

Human-in-the-loop

Quality is built into every dataset.

Automation increases speed. Human expertise protects quality. Every dataset moves through review before it's marked ready.

Automated pre-labeling gives annotators a head start, but nothing ships without a person checking it. Depending on the task, that can mean double annotation, reviewer consensus, or targeted sampling across a batch.
